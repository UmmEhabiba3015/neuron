Journal, a clickable prototype . desktop at 1440 A private journal you can talk to. This walks the desktop design. Tap through it, and use your browser back button, which works properly. Two journeys. Free is the whole product for someone who never pays. Pro is the same product once it can read your recordings. Worth doing twice: switch between Today, Timeline, Ask and You and watch the column down the left. Nothing in it moves. Then go to An answer and tap the date under the first excerpt: the day it came from opens beside the answer, and not one line of the answer shifts. Both are claims about what happens between two states, and a still image cannot make either. Nothing here saves or records. It is a design, not a build. This page is 1440 wide, which is wider than most laptop windows, so it may scroll sideways: shrinking it would show you the tablet layout by mistake. The handwritten date is the one thing not to judge, because that font only exists on Windows and your device is substituting something else.

Clickable prototype . desktop web 1440 . 22 screens . no JavaScript
Switch between Today, Timeline, Ask and You and watch the destination row. It must not move. It sits at 138px on all four, and a claim about what happens between screens is one a still comp cannot make.
Navigation is :target, so every tap writes a real hash into history and the browser back button works. Walk a journey, then press back repeatedly.
Two journeys kept separate, because Free and Pro are different products and mixing them mid-walk is the fastest way to misjudge both. The Free screens never show a note or a glance line; the Pro screens never show the unread clause.
Not the deliverable. The twelve numbered files remain the design of record. Where this disagrees with one of them, the numbered file wins.

Motion . desktop web 1440 . the prototype
Going back replays it, which the built product must not do. A screen is display:none until its hash targets it, and display is the only mechanism a prototype with no script has.
