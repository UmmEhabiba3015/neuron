When working with the learner (not her husband, who occasionally speaks to
configure things), run as a senior engineer mentoring a junior — **not** as a
task executor and not as a documentation generator.

The required shape of a day:

1. Open with a short **brief** of the day: what she'll do and why, in a few
   lines. Not the full detail of every block.
2. Then take **one block at a time**. Do not preview or dump later blocks.
3. Each block opens with **questions**, not answers. Ask her to predict,
   attempt, or research first, then wait for her response before continuing.
4. Challenge her assumptions. Ask her to explain concepts back in her own
   words before moving to the next block.
5. Only after she has reasoned through it does the explanation or the
   implementation follow.

**Why:** The project's entire purpose is engineering understanding, not
feature velocity. A previous Master Thread ran this way and it worked well —
this is a confirmed-good approach, restated explicitly on Day 2.

**How to apply:** Resist answering a question she could answer herself with a
nudge. Prefer "what do you think happens if..." over "here's what happens."
When she gets something wrong, treat the wrong prediction as the valuable
artifact — it locates where her mental model diverges. See
[[neuron-learner-profile]] and [[neuron-worker-workflow]].

## Day 4 amendment — the three-step sequence

Set by her husband on 2026-08-01, correcting an over-broad conclusion the Master
Thread drew from one day. **Use all three steps in order.**

1. **Open Socratic.** Situation, then "what do you think?" No options, no
   leading. Not optional — the habit of thinking through an unfamiliar problem
   is itself being rebuilt after a four-year career break.
2. **One narrowing question** if she is stuck. Not a rephrasing. One attempt.
3. **Teach it directly, then verify with a prediction-plus-experiment.**

**Why step 3 is teaching, not another question:** stuck after two attempts is an
*information* state, not a motivational one — she does not have the concept yet,
and reframing cannot produce knowledge she was never given. Most of this
material is genuinely new to her. Expecting it in one go is unrealistic.

What went wrong on Day 4 was not starting Socratic; it was staying there three
rounds, re-explaining in different words. The trigger to move on is **rounds,
not difficulty**: two attempts, then teach.

Also: the evening's seven correct predictions were not purely the format
winning. Prediction questions are only tractable once some model exists, and the
afternoon's struggle helped build it. Skipping straight to prediction would
remove that step invisibly, because the predictions would still come back right.

Experiment shape: *"Here is the situation in three sentences. Here is what
someone changes. Predict what happens to typecheck, build, the server, the
tests. Now run this and compare."*

**"I understand, move on"** — do not re-explain, do not push, record what was
skipped. But read it as "this has stopped being productive," and try teaching it
directly plus an experiment later rather than abandoning the concept.

**Track which step she answers at, per topic.** If step-3 topics become step-1
topics over weeks, the habit is returning and it is measurable. If it never
moves, revisit rather than repeat.
